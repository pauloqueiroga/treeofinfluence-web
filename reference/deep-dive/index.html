Deep-dive sub-tree

One node decomposed into its own tree.

Purpose

Zoom into one node of the full tree and give it its own tree: its causes, its parts, its sub-problems, or what solving it would take.

When to create it

  • A node is a complex decision that needs its own analysis.
  • A problem is big enough to deserve its own mapping session.
  • A subgroup has taken ownership of an area and needs space to work without disturbing the full tree.

These usually come up during Decompose.

What it contains

  • A header: "Deep Dive," followed by the name of the node being decomposed.
  • The parent node, at the entry point of the sub-tree, drawn with a bold double border to mark that it comes from the full tree.
  • The sub-tree around it: causes, components, or whatever the analysis needs.

A deep dive may use node types or arrow styles that the full tree does not. If it does, its own legend explains them.

Diagram as text: Deep dive on a problem from the onboarding tree

Nodes:

  • Root / Problem: "Questions in public channels go unanswered"
  • Root / Problem: "Past questions were met with impatience"
  • Root / Problem: "No one is assigned to help"
  • Root / Problem: "New hires are afraid to ask questions" (node from the full tree)
  • Action / Intervention: "Hold a weekly open question hour"
  • Desired State / Outcome: "Every question gets an answer within a day"

Arrows:

  • "Questions in public channels go unanswered" influences "New hires are afraid to ask questions"
  • "Past questions were met with impatience" influences "New hires are afraid to ask questions"
  • "No one is assigned to help" influences "New hires are afraid to ask questions"
  • "No one is assigned to help" influences "Hold a weekly open question hour"
  • "New hires are afraid to ask questions" influences "Every question gets an answer within a day"
  • "Hold a weekly open question hour" influences "Every question gets an answer within a day"

How it relates to the full tree

It supplements the parent node; it does not replace it. The node stays in the full tree, ideally with a link or comment pointing to the deep dive: "see Deep Dive: New hires are afraid to ask questions."

Example

In the team delivery gaps tree, the action "Assign a tech lead with a proven track record" became a deep dive that mapped the role's required characteristics, the strengths and weaknesses of candidates, and the role's key responsibilities. The group used it to make a hiring decision.

When you don't need it

Most nodes never need one. Deep-dive only the few nodes where understanding is weakest and the stakes are highest. Decomposing every node turns the technique into an endless recursion.

Orientation

When to draw the tree bottom-to-top and when left-to-right.

A tree can grow in 2 directions. Both are equally valid. Here is the same tree drawn both ways:

  • Root / Problem: "Senior people have no time to mentor"
  • Action / Intervention: "Assign each new hire a buddy"

Choosing one

Orientation Layout Best for
Vertical Roots at the bottom, outcomes at the top Wide, shallow trees: many problems and outcomes, short paths between them
Horizontal Roots on the left, outcomes on the right Narrow, deep trees: few problems and outcomes, long chains in between

Vertical trees spread wide layers across a widescreen display. Horizontal trees give long chains room to stretch.

Rules

  • Stay consistent within a view. Do not mix orientations in a single diagram.
  • Say which one you used. The legend states the flow, for example "roots (left) → outcomes (right)."
  • Switch if the tree fights you. If the tree keeps growing against its orientation, rearrange it. Moving nodes once is cheaper than fighting the layout for the rest of the tree's life.

Groups arrive at this convention on their own. In every observed tree, outcomes ended up aligned at one end and roots at the other, because that is what makes the arrows readable.

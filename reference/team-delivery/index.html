Example: Team delivery gaps

A team maps why it cannot deliver reliably, and decomposes one action / intervention into a role definition.

Context

One product team in a software organization could not deliver reliably. About 55% of the bugs it fixed were new regressions. The team had skill gaps and lacked technical leadership, talent was hard to retain, and the team was perceived as incompetent.

Goal

Understand why the team could not deliver reliably, and what would have to be true for that to change.

The outcomes the group wrote down say how it would know: fewer regressions, environments that match each other, and a team lead other leads hear from.

How the tree was built

The tree was built on a digital board, in sticky notes of 3 colors.

  • The board was pre-seeded with what was already known. That included a measurement, the share of fixed bugs that were new regressions, and the organizational context around the team. See Prepare.
  • The tree uses only the default relationship, influences. It has no artifact / deliverable nodes.
  • At least one action got a deep dive. "Assign a tech lead with a proven track record" was decomposed in its own area of the board.

The board also held frames that were not part of the tree, where the group planned who would be on which team. That planning stayed beside the tree, not on its nodes.

The deep dive

The action with the most at stake was assigning a tech lead. Who that person should be was a decision too complex for one node, so the group gave it a deep-dive sub-tree.

The deep dive was organized around the role, in 4 areas.

Diagram as text: The areas of the deep dive on assigning a tech lead

Nodes:

  • Action / Intervention: "Assign a tech lead with a proven track record" (node from the full tree)

No arrows yet.

It does not look like the full tree, and it does not have to. A deep dive takes whatever structure the question needs.

What it revealed

  • Measurement anchored the tree. "About 55% of fixed bugs are new regressions" is a statistic, and it is a problem: arrows leave it toward the actions. "Under 20% of fixed bugs are new," is an outcome. The group could see both the current state and the target on one board.
  • An outcome can be something you would notice."Other leads tell us the new lead has been talking to them" is not a number. It is one sign the group agreed to watch for.
  • Assigning a tech lead meant choosing a person, and a node could not hold that. The group used the deep dive to make a hiring decision.

Lessons

  • Pre-seed with data. A measurement on the board before the session starts gives participants a fact to react to, and it keeps the conversation concrete.
  • A node's type comes from its role, not its wording. A statistic can be a problem, and an overheard remark can be an outcome. See the core principles.
  • Use the group's vocabulary, and write it in the legend. The legend says what they mean, so the tree reads without help.
  • Deep-dive on the nodes that carry decisions.
  • Keep planning beside the tree, not in it. The hiring decision and the team composition used the tree as input. Neither became a node. See what it is and is not.

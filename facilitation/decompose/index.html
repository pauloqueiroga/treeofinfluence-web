Decompose

Break the highest-priority nodes into deep-dive sub-trees, in subgroups or as one group.

Purpose

Understand the few nodes that matter most in enough detail to act on them. Each one gets its own deep-dive sub-tree, built the same way as the main tree, around that single node.

Decompose is where the handoff from understanding to action begins. A first session may skip it, as the Quick Start does.

When it starts and ends

Decompose starts after Prioritize, with the top-voted nodes.

It ends when each subgroup understands its node well enough to inform what happens next. The goal is enough understanding to act, not a deep dive on every node.

Steps

  1. Choose the nodes. Take 2 to 5 of the top-voted nodes, favoring those where understanding is weakest and the stakes are highest.

  2. Organize the work. A large enough group splits into subgroups, each with one node and its own area of the board. A small group decomposes the nodes one after another, together.

  3. Start each sub-tree from its node. Copy the node from the full tree, mark it with a double border, and head the area "Deep Dive:" followed by the node's name.

  4. Ask what is inside it.

    What are the parts of this? What causes it? What does success here look like in detail?

  5. Run a short version of the session inside the sub-tree: seed, group, connect, and prioritize if needed. If the sub-tree uses node types or arrow styles the full tree does not, give it its own legend.

  6. Bring everyone back. Each subgroup shares what it found in a few minutes.

  7. Link each deep dive to its node in the full tree, with a note or a link such as "See Deep Dive: Most tickets are escalated to engineering." The deep dive supplements the node; it does not replace it.

Timing

Group size Suggested time What to expect
3-7 participants 20-30 min 1 or 2 deep dives, as one group
8-15 participants 30-40 min 2 or 3 deep dives, subgroups of 3 or 4 in parallel
16-30 participants 45-60 min 3 to 5 subgroups in parallel

A node that needs more than this deserves its own session. See running a deep-dive session.

Tips

  • Use the zoom question. Taking a node out of context and asking what would be at its roots often shows that one node was really several problems. See the zoom pattern.
  • Keep subgroups small. 3 or 4 people per subgroup lets everyone contribute.
  • Stop at understanding. A subgroup that starts assigning owners and dates has crossed into planning. That work is valuable, and it belongs after the session.

Traps

  • Decomposing every node. Deep dives on everything turn the technique into an endless recursion. Choose the few that matter.
  • Replacing the parent node. The deep dive explains a node of the full tree. The node stays where it is, with a link to its deep dive.

Example

In the renewals session, 3 subgroups of 4 took the 3 problems with the most votes. Sales and product took the sales promises, support took the escalations, and engineering took the outages.

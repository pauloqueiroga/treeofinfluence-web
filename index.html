Tree of Influence

A diagramming and facilitation technique for mapping how problems, actions, and desired outcomes influence each other.

A Tree of Influence is a map of a complex problem space, drawn with sticky notes and arrows. An individual or group of people builds it, and the conversation it creates is the point: people see how their problems connect, find shared causes, and agree on what matters before deciding what to do.

How to read a tree

  • Each sticky note is one node. Its color tells you its type: a root / problem, an action / intervention, a desired state / outcome, or an artifact / deliverable.
  • Each arrow means "this influences that." Follow the arrows from any problem to see what it affects.
  • The tree grows upward. Problems are the roots, at the bottom. Outcomes are the leaves, at the top. Actions are the branches in between. Trees can also grow from left to right; see orientation.
  • The legend says what everything means. Every tree has one.

The notation at a glance

Diagram as text: The notation at a glance
  • Root / Problem, marked [P]: something wrong or difficult.
  • Action / Intervention, marked [A]: a step between problems and outcomes.
  • Desired State / Outcome, marked [O]: a state the group wants to reach.
  • Artifact / Deliverable, marked [D]: something that exists or should exist.
  • Influences arrow, solid: the default arrow.
  • Blocks / Impedes arrow, dashed: prevents the target.
  • Attacks arrow, dotted: an artifact targets a problem.

That is the whole visual vocabulary: 4 colors of sticky note and 3 kinds of arrow. Most trees use only 3 colors and the plain arrow. See colors and shapes and arrow styles.

A Tree of Influence is a tool for understanding problems. It should not be mistaken for a project plan, a backlog, a decision record, or a status tracker. It can feed all of those, but its job ends when the group understands the problem space well enough to act. Read more about its scope.
